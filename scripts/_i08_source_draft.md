## UNRESOLVED

Items the brief named that I could NOT verify, with exactly what is doubtful:

1. **The OSU Press "Cornell series" of Trollopes.** Four searches found no such series. The
   "Cornell edition" that does exist in nineteenth-century scholarship is the Cornell Wordsworth
   series and Cornell Studies in English generally — not Trollope, and not Ohio State UP. Verified
   replacement used above: *Selected Works of Anthony Trollope*, 62 vols, gen. ed. N. John Hall,
   Arno Press, 1981.
2. **The "Harvard/Atlantic edition" of Henry James.** No such scholarly edition exists under that
   name. Verified replacements used above: the New York Edition (Scribner's, 1907–09) and *The
   Complete Letters of Henry James* (Nebraska, 2006– ). The James biography/letters originally
   associated with Houghton Mifflin are not a "Harvard/Atlantic" edition.
3. **"Yale and Kentucky editions of Whitman."** Yale UP does not publish a Whitman scholarly
   edition, and no University Press of Kentucky Whitman edition could be traced. Verified
   replacements: *Leaves of Grass: A Textual Variorum of the Printed Poems* (NYU Press, 1979–80) and
   *The Walt Whitman Archive* (Nebraska).
4. **"California edition of Dickinson."** No such edition of the poems exists; the University of
   California Press Dickinson title I could trace is Barbara Hapgood's *Dickinson's Prosody* —
   a study, not a text. Verified replacements used above: Johnson (Belknap, 1955) and Franklin
   (Belknap, 1998).
5. **Wordsworth Classical Poets series titles** (e.g. a Classical Poets Browning/Hopkins volume):
   publisher confirmed as Wordsworth Editions, but I could not pin an editor and year for any
   specific Victorian title, so no row was used.
6. **Generic "Penguin and W. W. Norton standard editions".** Both houses publish many Victorian
   titles; without a named editor/series I could not verify a specific volume, so only the Norton
   Critical Editions with named editors are used as rows.
7. **Doubtful facts inside otherwise verified rows** (each flagged `check_first` in the table):
   Browning Longman volume count; Clarendon Brontë volume editors/dates; Meredith OWC 2nd edn year;
   Gissing Collected Letters volume count; Hardy 2012 supplement numbering; Whitman Archive founding
   year; Emerson CW vol. I year; Moby-Dick NNME title-page editor order; Alcott LOA volume number;
   Jack London LOA volume editor (none printed on the volume); NAAL 9th edn per-volume editors;
   Columbia Literary History contributing editors; Swinburne 1866 imprint (Moxon vs Hotten).
   For the RESERVE items the doubtful facts are the Victorian Web launch year, the Dickens Journals
   Online credited editors and the Jane Eyre/Wuthering Heights Clarendon volume dates.
8. **Arnold Bennett, *A Man from the North*** (London: John Lane, 1898) is real and was checked, but
   the imprint is recorded inconsistently (John Lane vs The Bodley Head, which was Lane's list), so
   the publisher fact is not firm enough for the table; it stays in RESERVE with that caveat.

## CORRECTIONS MADE

Real checking, not recall — every one of these was my first guess and was falsified:

1. **Norton Anthology of English Literature, Victorian volume.** Guessed Vol. D. It is **Vol. E:
   The Victorian Age (1830–1901)**; Vol. D is Romantic (1785–1832). Also, the current 11th edn is
   **2024**, with **Catherine Robson and Rachel Ablow** as the Victorian volume editors alongside
   Greenblatt — not the older Marshall/Patten editorships.
2. **Darwin's *Annotated Origin*.** Guessed the annotator was Janet Browne. It is **James T. Costa**
   (Belknap Press of Harvard UP, 2009). Browne is the author of a Darwin biography, a different book.
3. **Dickens's Pilgrim letters.** Guessed 14 vols ending 1973. Confirmed **12 vols, Clarendon Press,
   1965–2002**.
4. **Pater's *Renaissance* (1893 text).** Guessed an Oxford or Princeton edition dated 1971. It is
   **Donald L. Hill, University of California Press, 1980**.
5. **Thoreau's scholarly edition.** Guessed Yale University Press for *The Writings of Henry D.
   Thoreau*. It is **Princeton University Press**, editor-in-chief **Elizabeth Witherell**; Yale
   publishes only Cramer's *Walden: A Fully Annotated Edition* (2004).
6. **Henry James's scholarly correspondence.** Guessed "Harvard/Atlantic" (Houghton Mifflin). It is
   **University of Nebraska Press, 2006–**, gen. eds. **Pierre A. Walker and Greg W. Zacharias**.
7. **Elizabeth Barrett Browning's *Aurora Leigh*.** Guessed Norton. Verified alternatives are
   **Margaret Reynolds, Ohio University Press, 1992** and **Cora Kaplan, Women's Press, 1978**; and
   the 5-vol scholarly *Works* is **Pickering & Chatto, 2010**, gen. ed. **Sandra Donaldson**.
8. **Carlyle–Emerson correspondence.** Guessed Chapman & Hall, 1882. It is **Chatto & Windus, 1883**,
   ed. **Charles Eliot Norton** (2 vols; Boston: Ticknor).
9. **Morris's *Collected Works*.** Guessed 23 vols. It is **24 vols**, ed. **May Morris**, Longmans,
   Green, **1910–1915**.
10. **Tennyson's standard scholarly text.** Guessed a single-volume Ricks of 1969. The working
    edition is the **2nd edn, 3 vols, Longman Annotated English Poets, 1987** (1st edn 1969).
11. **The Trollope scholarly set.** Guessed an "Ohio State Press Cornell series". Verified as
    **Arno Press, 1981**, 62 vols, gen. ed. **N. John Hall**; "Cornell edition" in the nineteenth-
    century bibliographic literature refers to the Wordsworth series, not Trollope.
12. **Whitman and Dickinson anchors.** Guessed "Yale and Kentucky" for Whitman (it is **NYU Press**,
    *Collected Writings*, 1979–80, Bradley/Blodgett/Golden/White) and "California" for Dickinson
    (it is **Belknap/Harvard**: Johnson 1955, Franklin 1998).
13. **George Eliot's letters.** Guessed a companion to the *Works*; the letters are **Haight, Yale
    UP, 1954–1978**, and Middlemarch's current scholarly anthology text is the **Norton Critical
    Edition ed. Ronjaunee Chatterjee, 2024** (not the 1965 Rosenfield/1972 edition).
14. **ODNB editorship.** Recalled "Matthew and Harrison" plus a later revision: the 60-vol 2004 set
    is Matthew & Harrison, and **Lawrence Goldman succeeded Harrison in October 2004**, so any
    "revised edition" claim needs the successor named correctly rather than appended casually.
