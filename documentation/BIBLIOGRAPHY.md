# Bibliography

Catalog of sources recorded in the `sources` table of the master database.
This file is updated after every installment. Entries mirror the database
records (title, author/editor, publisher, year, type, reliability notes).
URLs are listed only where verified stable. No references, dates, URLs, or
DOIs are invented; unverified fields are left null.

## Installment 1 — Old English and Early Medieval Literature (c. 450–1100)

### Primary editions and translations

- **The Anglo-Saxon Poetic Records: A Collective Edition** — George Philip
  Krapp and Elliott Van Kirk Dobbie (eds.), Columbia University Press,
  1931–1953. *Edition.* The standard six-volume edition of the complete Old
  English poetic corpus.
- **Anglo-Saxon Poetry** — S. A. J. Bradley (trans.), J. M. Dent (Everyman),
  1982. *Edition.* Complete prose translation of the poetic corpus; used for
  plot summaries and structural description.
- **Beowulf: A Verse Translation** — Seamus Heaney (trans.), Daniel Donoghue
  (ed.), W. W. Norton, 2002. *Edition.* Influential modern verse translation
  with critical apparatus.
- **The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle: A Revised Translation** — Dorothy Whitelock,
  David C. Douglas, and Susie I. Tucker (eds.); Michael Swanton (rev. trans.),
  Routledge, 1996. *Edition.* Standard modern translation of all major
  Chronicle manuscripts.
- **English Historical Documents, Volume I: c. 500–1042** — Dorothy Whitelock
  (ed.), Eyre and Spottiswoode, 1955. *Edition.* Standard collection of
  translated primary documents (chronicles, laws, charters, letters).

### Reference works

- **The Blackwell Encyclopaedia of Anglo-Saxon England, 2nd edition** —
  Michael Lapidge, John Blair, Simon Keynes, and Donald Scragg (eds.),
  Wiley-Blackwell, 2014. *Encyclopedia.* Comprehensive scholarly reference
  for persons, places, texts, and institutions of the period.
- **The Cambridge Companion to Old English Literature, 2nd edition** —
  Malcolm Godden and Michael Lapidge (eds.), Cambridge University Press,
  2013. *Reference.* Authoritative survey essays on all major genres, authors,
  and contexts.
- **Dictionary of Old English: A to I online** — Antonette diPaolo Healey et
  al. (eds.), Dictionary of Old English Project, University of Toronto, 2007–.
  *Reference.* The standard lexicographical resource for Old English.
  URL: <https://tapor.library.utoronto.ca/doe/> (accessed 2026-11-03).
- **British Library Digitised Manuscripts** — British Library, 2010–.
  *Archive.* Digital facsimiles of Cotton manuscripts, including the Nowell
  Codex (Beowulf) and related items.
  URL: <https://www.bl.uk/manuscripts/> (accessed 2026-11-03).

### Anthologies and surveys

- **The Norton Anthology of English Literature, 10th edition, Volume A: The
  Middle Ages** — Stephen Greenblatt (general editor); James Simpson and
  Julie Orlemanski (eds.), W. W. Norton, 2018. *Anthology.* Starting
  framework for period coverage; texts in modern translation with
  authoritative introductions.

### Textbooks

- **A Guide to Old English, 8th edition** — Bruce Mitchell and Fred C.
  Robinson, Wiley-Blackwell, 2011. *Textbook.* Standard textbook of the
  language with texts; used for linguistic and textual verification.

  English Compendium, University of Michigan Press / University of Michigan

## Installment 3 — Renaissance and Early Tudor (c. 1500–1558)

### Reference works and surveys

- **The Cambridge History of Early Modern English Literature** — David
  Loewenstein and Janel Mueller (eds.), Cambridge University Press, 2002.
  *Reference.* The standard collaborative history of the literature of
  Britain from the Reformation to the Restoration.
- **The Oxford Handbook of Tudor Literature, 1485–1603** — Mike Pincombe and
  Cathy Shrank (eds.), Oxford University Press, 2009. *Reference.*
  Authoritative survey essays on the full range of Tudor writing.
- **English Literature in the Sixteenth Century, Excluding Drama** — C. S.
  Lewis, Clarendon Press (Oxford History of English Literature), 1954.
  *Reference.* Classic single-author survey of the non-dramatic literature
  of the century.
- **The Norton Anthology of English Literature, 10th edition, Volume B: The
  Sixteenth Century and the Early Seventeenth Century** — Stephen Greenblatt
  (gen. ed.), W. W. Norton, 2018. *Edition.* Standard classroom anthology;
  used for canonical texts and apparatus.
- **Oxford Dictionary of National Biography** — H. C. G. Matthew and Brian
  Harrison (eds.), Oxford University Press, 2004 (online edition). *Reference.*
  Authoritative biographical entries; carried over from Installment 2.

### Period studies

- **Tudor Drama before Shakespeare, 1485–1590: New Directions for Research,
  Criticism, and Pedagogy** — Lloyd Edward Kermode, Jason Scott-Warren, and
  Martine van Elk (eds.), Palgrave Macmillan, 2004. *Reference.* Survey of
  the interludes, moralities, and academic plays preceding the public stage.
- **The King's Reformation: Henry VIII and the Remaking of the English
  Church** — G. W. Bernard, Yale University Press, 2005. *Reference.*
  Standard modern account of the Henrician Reformation and its politics.
- **Poetry and Politics in the English Renaissance, revised edition** —
  David Norbrook, Oxford University Press, 2002. *Reference.* Study of the
  political contexts of early modern English poetry from Skelton to Marvell.

### Primary editions

- **Sir Thomas Wyatt: The Complete Poems** — R. A. Rebholz (ed.), Penguin,
  1978. *Edition.* Standard modern edition of Wyatt's verse.
- **The Book of Common Prayer: The Texts of 1549, 1559, and 1662** — Brian
  Cummings (ed.), Oxford University Press, 2011. *Edition.* Standard
  scholarly edition of the three foundational Prayer Books.

## Installment 4 — Elizabethan and Jacobean (1558–1625)

### Reference works and surveys

- **The Cambridge Companion to Shakespeare** — Margreta de Grazia and Stanley
  Wells (eds.), Cambridge University Press, 2001. *Reference.* Critical essays
  on Shakespeare's works and their contexts.
- **The Cambridge Companion to English Renaissance Drama, 2nd edition** —
  A. R. Braunmuller and Michael Hattaway (eds.), Cambridge University Press,
  2003. *Reference.* The standard collaborative reference for the
  non-Shakespearean drama of 1558–1642.
- **The Cambridge Companion to Christopher Marlowe** — Patrick Cheney (ed.),
  Cambridge University Press, 2004. *Reference.* Essays on Marlowe's plays,
  poems, and translations.
- **The Cambridge Companion to Ben Jonson** — Richard Harp and Stanley
  Stewart (eds.), Cambridge University Press, 2000. *Reference.* Essays on
  Jonson's drama, poetry, masques, and criticism.
- **The Cambridge Companion to John Donne** — Achsah Guibbory (ed.),
  Cambridge University Press, 2006. *Reference.* Essays on Donne's poetry,
  prose, and sermons.
- **The Cambridge Companion to Spenser** — Andrew Hadfield (ed.), Cambridge
  University Press, 2001. *Reference.* Essays on Spenser's poetry and prose.

### Primary editions

- **The Riverside Shakespeare, 2nd edition** — G. Blakemore Evans (ed.) with
  J. J. M. Tobin, Houghton Mifflin, 1997. *Edition.* The standard American
  one-volume complete works; used for play texts, dates, and the collaborative
  canon.
- **The Oxford Shakespeare: The Complete Works, 2nd edition** — Stanley Wells
  and Gary Taylor (eds.), Clarendon Press, 2005. *Edition.* The standard
  British complete works.
