## Method

**Scope.** 1 January 1946 to 31 December 2000 (decision of 2026-11-06). The core window is 1951–2000, and 1946–1950 is reported separately as the bridge from installment 9.
- **Traditions covered:** British (English, Scottish, Welsh), Irish and Northern Irish, and American literature. Also the English-language literatures of Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the Caribbean, Africa and South Asia, and their diasporas.
- **Inclusion test:** literary activity and publication in the period, not birth date or fame.
- **Theorists in translation:** non-English-language theorists named in Part VII (Barthes, Derrida, Foucault, Lacan and others) are admitted as theory in translation. Their records give the original language, and they are distinguished from critics writing in English.

**Records.**
- **Works.** Every literary work gets its own work record, with depth tiered by significance:
  - *major:* the full seven-part record, with a plot or argument of several paragraphs;
  - *significant:* every analytical field, shorter;
  - *minor:* shorter but specific.
- **Seven-part mapping:**
  - overview → `description`
  - content → `summary`
  - summary or argument → `plot_or_argument`
  - themes → `thematic_analysis`
  - literary characteristics → `narrative_structure` and `meter_and_prosody`
  - significance → `literary_significance`
  - critical context → `critical_context`
- **Bibliographic and verification fields:**
  - `bibliographical_notes` holds the first-edition details.
  - `research_notes` holds unresolved bibliographical questions.
  - `verification_status` records what has actually been checked.
- **Authors** carry a `bibliography_status` (complete, substantially complete or incomplete) and a written statement of what the bibliography covers, how it was checked and what is still missing.
- **Shared authors.** A writer recorded by an earlier installment keeps that single author record; installment-10 works link to it.

**Strict build.** `META["strict"]` turns off all template enrichment. `scripts/strict_checks.py` blocks the build when:
- a field falls short of its depth tier;
- boilerplate appears;
- a sentence is repeated across works;
- a date falls outside 1946–2000;
- a taxonomy name is undefined;
- a record is marked verified without the sources actually consulted.

No source is attached to records wholesale. Installments 8–9 attached four general reference works to every record; installment 10 does not.

**Discovery baseline.** `scripts/i10_discovery.py` builds `research/installment_10/discovery_inventory.csv` from the peer-reviewed datasets of the Post45 Data Collective (Emory University):
- US literary prizes, with Wikidata, VIAF and LCCN identifiers;
- British prizes, 1990–2022;
- NYT hardcover fiction bestsellers;
- the Asian American literary canon;
- NEA literature fellowships;
- the Iowa Writers' Workshop: graduates with a library authority record, and the HathiTrust works of Workshop authors;
- *Playboy* fiction and speculative-fiction novels, used for discovery only because the repository index does not document them.

**Curated lists** in `research/installment_10/lists/` supplement the datasets; each list's source is a record in `data/sources/installment_10_sources.json`. So far there are four:
- the Booker Prize winners and shortlists 1969–2000, with the 1970 "Lost Booker" shortlist and longlist (205 rows, 140 authors);
- the Heinemann African Writers Series, 1962–2003 (641 rows);
- the Commonwealth Writers' Prize, 1987–2000: regional and overall Best Book and Best First Book winners, and the Commonwealth Short Story Competition winners (112 rows);
- the Heinemann Caribbean Writers Series as Wikipedia lists it (33 rows; a selection, not the series' bibliography).

Each candidate is assigned to the order's decades and given a priority tier: P1 for major prizes and the most-studied or best-selling authors, P2 for other prizes, shortlists and single bestsellers, and P3 for fellowships, periodical contributors and prolific genre authors. An inventory row is evidence of activity, not an inclusion decision.

**Verification levels.**
- *verified:* dates and bibliography cross-checked in at least two independent sources, listed with the record.
- *partially verified:* bibliographic facts checked, but the summary or critical text written from knowledge of the work and not checked against a source in this pass.
- *unverified:* not checked.

## 15. Remaining limitations and unresolved issues

1. **Coverage is at the very beginning.** Installment 10 holds 77 works by 8 authors: waves 01–04 (Ellison, Larkin, Hansberry and Murray), wave 05 (Orwell, whose record belongs to installment 9), wave 06 (Amos Tutuola, the first author from outside the British and American traditions), wave 07 (George Lamming, the first Caribbean author) and wave 08 (Samuel Selvon, the first Indo-Caribbean author). The discovery baseline lists 6,122 candidate authors, and the movement, genre and theme taxonomies are defined, but almost all of the reconstruction is still to do.
2. **Gaps in the baseline itself.** The Post45 datasets are overwhelmingly American, and their British data begin in 1990. The Booker list now covers British, Irish and Commonwealth *novels* from 1969, but only novels by Commonwealth and Irish citizens; it contains no poetry, drama or criticism.
   - **Evidence:** none of the three authors added in waves 02–04 was in the inventory: Larkin (poetry), Hansberry (drama) and Murray (criticism and jazz writing). The inventory therefore cannot serve as the denominator of completeness for poetry, drama or criticism.
   - **Further evidence (wave 06):** Amos Tutuola is not in the inventory either, although the movement taxonomy dates African literature in English from his first book (1952). The prize-based baseline misses African, Caribbean and South Asian writing before the Booker list begins in 1969. A publishers'-series inventory (for example Heinemann's African Writers Series) and the national bibliographies are needed for those traditions.
   - **African list added (2026-11-06):** the Heinemann African Writers Series now covers African writing in English published in that series, 1962–2000 (313 candidates). It omits authors published elsewhere (Tutuola, published by Faber, appears only as an anthology contributor), and its years are those of the series edition, not of first publication. 
   - **Further evidence (wave 07):** George Lamming is in no inventory. Caribbean and South Asian writing before 1969 still has no list; the Heinemann Caribbean Writers Series and national bibliographies are the next candidates.
   - **Caribbean and Commonwealth lists added (2026-11-06):** the Commonwealth Writers' Prize (Best Book 1987–2000, Best First Book 1989–2000 and the short-story winners; 99 candidates) and Wikipedia's selection from the Heinemann Caribbean Writers Series (33 titles; 16 candidates of 1946–2000). Neither reaches the 1950s and 1960s well: the prize begins in 1987, and the series table names neither Lamming nor Selvon.
   - **Further evidence (wave 08):** Samuel Selvon is in no inventory either. Caribbean writing of the 1950s and 1960s, and all South Asian writing, still need lists: a bibliography of West Indian fiction (Kenneth Ramchand's *The West Indian Novel and Its Background*, 1970, is one to check), the Sahitya Akademi Award for English and the national bibliographies.
   - **Planned inventories:**
     - Whitbread from 1971, the Hawthornden Prize, the James Tait Black and Somerset Maugham awards, the Cholmondeley Award, the Queen's Gold Medal for Poetry, and the Carnegie and Kate Greenaway Medals;
     - the Governor General's Awards, the Miles Franklin Award, the New Zealand book awards, the Sahitya Akademi Award for English and the Noma Award (the Commonwealth Writers' Prize is done);
     - the Hugo, Nebula, Edgar, CWA Dagger, Newbery, Caldecott, Lambda, New York Drama Critics' Circle, Tony and Olivier awards;
     - the movement anthologies: *New Lines*, *The New American Poetry*, *Black Fire*, *Aiiieeeee!*, *The Mersey Sound*, *Mirrorshades*, *In the American Tree* and Granta 8.
3. **Name variants and namesakes in the inventory.** Surname-first forms are now converted and Iowa middle-initial variants merged (section 13), but other variants remain, for example "Angela Thirkell" and "Angela Mackail Thirkell", and "Ogawa, Ai" beside "Ai". Names are folded only by punctuation and accents, so variants are merged by hand when a wave reaches them. The reverse also happens: two writers with the same name share one row. "David Cook" joins the editor of three Makerere anthologies in the African Writers Series with a 1977 E. M. Forster Award winner, who is not shown to be the same person.
4. **Translated foreign authors** (for example Camus and Moravia) appear among the bestsellers. They are out of scope unless they are theorists covered by Part VII.
5. **Partial verification.**
   - The 43 work records of waves 01–05 are *partially verified*: their bibliographic facts were checked, but their plot and critical texts were not checked against a source in this pass.
   - Of wave 06's 11 records, 4 are *verified*, their plots corroborated by two or more reference sources (*The Palm-Wine Drinkard*, *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts*, *Simbi* and *The Witch-Herbalist*). 7 are *partially verified*, their content known only from a publisher's description or a single summary (*The Brave African Huntress*, *Feather Woman of the Jungle*, *Ajaiyi*, *The Wild Hunter*, *Yoruba Folktales*, *Pauper, Brawler and Slanderer* and *The Village Witch Doctor*). None of the eleven books was read.
   - Open bibliographic questions are written into the records' research notes. Examples: the first-edition publisher of *A Raisin in the Sun*; the 1971 or 1972 date of *South to a Very Old Place*; the publisher of *The Movement* (1964); the network behind *The Drinking Gourd*; the lecture series behind *The Hero and the Blues*.
   - Orwell's sources disagree on several points, each recorded on the record concerned: the Wikipedia bibliography dates the American *Such, Such Were the Joys* 1958 (Harcourt, Brace published it on 26 February 1953) and names a publisher that did not yet exist; the preface's typescript is said to have been found in 1971 or in 1972; "I Write As I Please" appears in WorldCat but not in orwell.ru's lists; the two orwell.ru lists differ on the contents of the 1953 collection; the *Collected Essays, Journalism and Letters* is dated "1968" or "1968–1970".
6. **Unresolved attributions:** *Reflections on Logic, Politics, and Reality* (1989), listed under Albert Murray by Gale and the Alabama Authors database, and *The Fantasy Poets No. 21* (1954, contents unverified) are named in the authors' bibliography notes but have no work records.

"Politics and the English Language" (1946) has no record of its own because it is collected in *Shooting an Elephant and Other Essays* (1950), whose record lists it.
8. **Audits 3 and 8** (the external bibliographic comparison and the independent second pass) have not been run.
9. **Accuracy of installments 8–9.** Checking other things turned up four installment-9 records with wrong plots: three were invented (*Pastors and Masters*, *The Nebuly Coat* and *The Fashion in Shrouds*), and *Surfeit of Lampreys* was wrong on motive and murderer. All four were in the late batches. An installment-8 record also had unsupported claims (Fuller's dispatches). All five were corrected from sources. Worse, the later installment-9 batches also contained a **fabricated work**: "Picture of Mortimer and Ray", attributed to Malcolm Muggeridge, which matches no book and has been removed. Every installment 8–9 title therefore needs checking against an external bibliography, not only every plot. Separately, Ngaio Marsh's bibliography gave London dates for six novels first published in Boston or Auckland, and the same error may affect other authors published first in the United States. Finding them incidentally means more are likely: a record-by-record check of plots and claims in installments 8–9 against sources, starting with the later installment-9 batches, is outstanding and belongs to Audit 5 run database-wide. Installments 8–9 also attach general reference works to their records (Fuller's American dispatches carry the Victorian volume of the *Norton Anthology of English Literature* among four); these show where facts can be checked, not that each record was checked against them.
10. **The importer never deletes.** It upserts works by title, authorship status and composition start, so correcting a composition date on an existing record leaves the old row behind (this happened to *Pastors and Masters*). After such corrections the database must be rebuilt from the JSON (an empty file, `alembic upgrade head`, then `import_data.py`); `check_duplicates.py` lists any row that no installment file defines.
11. **Earlier-installment authors.** The method is settled: an author keeps the single record of the installment that first recorded them, installment 10 links its works to that record, and the bibliography statement reaches it through `scripts/content/i10_extensions.py`, merged when the earlier installment is built. Orwell is the only author done so far.
